# Abstract

Agrogeophysics employs geophysical methods - from electromagnetic and electrical techniques to seismic and remote sensing approaches - ranging from ground-based systems, through drones, to satellite-borne equipment, to characterize the spatial and temporal variability of soil and subsurface processes relevant for agronomic management. These methods provide non-invasive, scalable, and high-resolution insights into the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum, supporting improved understanding of water dynamics, soil properties, and subsurface heterogeneity. 
The 4th Agrogeophysics seminar welcomes contributions addressing the use of geophysical methods: - for soil characterization, irrigation management, and monitoring of agro-environmental processes; - the development of innovative acquisition systems, including autonomous and drone-based platforms; - and the integration of geophysical data with modeling approaches such as geostatistics, data assimilation, and artificial intelligence. 
We encourage submissions presenting advances in proximal and remote sensing for soil–water–plant interactions; studies linking geophysical measurements to soil physical and hydrological properties through pedophysical relationships; and novel methodologies for improving interpretation, inversion, and uncertainty quantification. Contributions demonstrating real-world applications, operational workflows, and decision-support tools for agricultural management are especially welcome, highlighting the growing role of agrogeophysics in addressing global challenges related to food security, water resources, and environmental sustainability.
